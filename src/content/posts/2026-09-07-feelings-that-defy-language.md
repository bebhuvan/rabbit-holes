Language is probably one of the greatest inventions of mankind.

I don’t think there would have been civilization, at least not in any recognizable form, without our ability to speak to one another, communicate, pass things down, argue, explain, warn, comfort, persuade, remember.

And despite the fact that we’ve been doing this for God knows how long, it still surprises me that there are so many feelings and emotions that seem to run up against the limits of language.

There’s this beautiful line by Tracy K. Smith that I keep coming back to:

“Poetry is the language that sits really close to feelings that defy language.”

And I think about this quite a bit because I suffer from debilitating sleep issues, which have gotten worse in recent months.

The thing with not getting enough sleep is that it doesn’t just make you tired.

It messes with your emotional balance. Your baseline changes. And the longer it goes on, the worse it gets.

There are days when I’m unable to think clearly. There are days when I’m unable to express myself clearly. And there are days when I don’t even know how to describe what exactly is happening inside my head.

This is quite scary, at least to me.

Whenever I speak to friends or colleagues about this and they ask me, “How do you even function without sleep?”, I end up using metaphors.

I feel like a zombie.

I feel underwater.

I feel like I’m moving through sludge.

And I don’t say these things because I’m trying to make it sound dramatic. I say them because I genuinely don’t know a clean way to describe how I feel using plain old English.

Maybe I just don’t know enough words. But I don’t think that’s the problem.

This is just one example.

I have a lot of friends who struggle with mental health issues, and sometimes when I ask them how they are feeling, I can see the struggle on their face.

They are trying to find the words.

And often they can’t.

Forget explaining what caused the feeling. Sometimes it is hard enough to describe the feeling itself.

I relate to this.

There have been times when I’ve felt so blue that the only way I’ve been able to describe it is as drowning, with the weight of the water increasing every minute.

Or it feels like somebody is pushing me underwater with a force much stronger than me, and I’m unable to pull myself out.

Of course, neither description is quite right.

But “sad” isn’t right either.

“Low” isn’t right.

Even words like “anxious” or “depressed” can sometimes feel too broad, too clean, or too inadequate for whatever messy thing is actually happening inside your head.

I love words. A ridiculous amount of my life revolves around the written word and the spoken word.

So I tend to pay attention to how people speak. The words they choose. The phrases they invent. The metaphors they reach for. The way they sometimes pause halfway through a sentence because whatever word they were looking for simply isn’t there.

And I’m reasonably certain that we are doing this all the time.

We are straining at the limits of language.

I don’t think this has much to do with vocabulary or grammar or whether somebody is articulate enough.

I think the full spectrum of human experience is simply much larger than the meanings that our humble words can comfortably contain.

Maybe this is one reason why we invented metaphors.

Maybe this is one reason poetry exists.

Maybe this is also one reason talking about mental health is so difficult. Before another person can understand what you’re feeling, you first have to somehow convert this shapeless thing happening inside you into words.

And that conversion is imperfect.

Sometimes wildly so.

Which is a fascinating paradox, at least to me.

Language is the thing that lets us share our inner lives with one another.

And yet our inner lives are often much larger than language.
