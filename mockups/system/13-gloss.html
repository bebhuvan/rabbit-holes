The antimeme is the message of the medium

Kevin Munger · kevinmunger.substack.com

Memes, antimemes, and what a feed cannot carry

Written by a machine

Claude Opus 4.8 wrote this gloss from the source above, at my request. It is here so I remember the piece, not because I wrote it. Where it is wrong, the source is right.

Why I asked for this

I don’t always understand half of what he says.

Munger’s argument turns on a distinction that sounds like wordplay and isn’t. A meme, in the original Dawkins sense, is a unit of culture that spreads — an idea shaped by selection pressure toward being transmitted. On a feed-based platform, that pressure is extreme, and what survives is whatever compresses best into an image, a phrase, a clip.

An antimeme is not the opposite of that. It is something that resists the compression. Not because it is obscure or difficult, but because the thing itself is made of properties a feed cannot encode.

The information-density argument

The move that makes the essay more than a taxonomy is the claim about density. Munger accepts that a meme may be the densest possible communication within a feed — and then points out how thin that frame is:

Humans are quite plastic; our sensory apparatus changes based on the communication environment in which we are raised. But we’re not infinitely plastic. The information-density frontier must involve all of our senses.

The densest human communication, on this account, happens when the stakes are high and you are physically among other people. Team sports. The school dance. The street protest. These are saturated with signal — posture, timing, breath, the reactions of dozens of people at once — and essentially none of it survives being filmed.

Why this is a claim about media, not about touching grass

It would be easy to read this as nostalgia. It isn’t. The argument is structural: every medium has a carrying capacity, and a medium selects for the content that fits it. As more of our attention moves into feeds, we are not just consuming worse things — we are being trained to treat the feed’s carrying capacity as the natural limit of what communication is.

The loss, then, is not that antimemes are being outcompeted. It is that they become progressively harder to notice, because the medium that dominates your attention has no way to represent them, and therefore no way to remind you they exist.

The one thing worth pushing back on

The essay is stronger on diagnosis than on measurement. “Information density” is doing heavy lifting without a definition that would let you compare a protest to a tweet in any rigorous way. The intuition is persuasive; the metric is gestural. That may be fine for the argument’s purpose, but it is the seam where a sceptic would pull.
